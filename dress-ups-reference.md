# Dress-Ups & Sentence Openers Pocket Reference

[← Back to Homework Study]({{ '/' | relative_url }})

*A quick guide for making writing clearer, stronger, and more interesting.*

## Dress-Ups

### 1. Quality Adjective

| Category | Content |
|---|---|
| **Remember** | Describes a noun by telling what kind or what it is like. |
| **Formula** | **quality adjective + noun** |
| **Word Bank** | colorful, tiny, enormous, shiny, brave, gentle, friendly, fluffy, ancient, delicious |
| **Examples** | The **colorful** bird sang.<br>The **tiny** ant carried a leaf.<br>The **friendly** dog wagged its tail.<br>We crossed the **ancient** bridge. |
| **Look For** | A specific word that helps the reader picture a person, place, animal, or thing. |

### 2. Strong Verb

| Category | Content |
|---|---|
| **Remember** | Replaces a weak or ordinary verb with a precise action word. |
| **Formula** | **subject + strong verb** |
| **Word Bank** | sprinted, dashed, whispered, shouted, pounced, grabbed, soared, rescued, wandered, blasted |
| **Examples** | The lion **pounced** on its prey.<br>Dad **whispered** the secret.<br>Emma **sprinted** to school.<br>The eagle **soared** above the mountains. |
| **Look For** | Weak verbs such as *went, got, said, made,* or *looked* that can be replaced. |

### 3. -ly Adverb

| Category | Content |
|---|---|
| **Remember** | Tells how an action happens. |
| **Formula** | **verb + -ly adverb** |
| **Word Bank** | quickly, carefully, happily, quietly, proudly, slowly, gently, eagerly, softly, bravely |
| **Examples** | She smiled **happily**.<br>Dad drove **carefully**.<br>The class listened **quietly**.<br>The soldier fought **bravely**. |
| **Look For** | A word ending in **-ly** that answers, “How did it happen?” |

### 4. Because Clause

| Category | Content |
|---|---|
| **Remember** | Explains why something happened. |
| **Formula** | **complete thought + because + reason** |
| **Useful Word** | because |
| **Examples** | We stayed inside **because it rained**.<br>She smiled **because she won**.<br>He wore a jacket **because it was cold**.<br>We hurried home **because it was getting dark**. |
| **Look For** | A clear reason that answers, “Why?” |

### 5. Who/Which Clause

| Category | Content |
|---|---|
| **Remember** | Adds information about a noun. Use **who** for people and **which** for things or animals. |
| **Formula** | **noun, who/which + extra information, + rest of sentence** |
| **Useful Words** | who, which |
| **Examples** | Mia, **who loves animals**, adopted a puppy.<br>Mr. Lee, **who teaches science**, showed us a fossil.<br>The bicycle, **which had a flat tire**, leaned against the wall.<br>The owl, **which hunts at night**, rested in the tree. |
| **Look For** | A person or thing that could be described with extra information. |

### 6. WWW.ASIA Clause

| Category | Content |
|---|---|
| **Remember** | Begins with a subordinating word and cannot stand alone. |
| **Formula** | **WWW.ASIA clause, complete thought** |
| **Word Bank** | when, while, where, as, since, if, although |
| **Examples** | **When the bell rang**, the students hurried outside.<br>**While Mom cooked**, I set the table.<br>**Since the trail was muddy**, we walked carefully.<br>**Although he was nervous**, Ben gave his speech. |
| **Look For** | A dependent clause joined to a complete thought. Add a comma when the clause comes first. |

## Sentence Openers

### 7. Subject Opener (S)

| Category | Content |
|---|---|
| **Remember** | Starts directly with the person, place, animal, or thing doing the action. |
| **Formula** | **subject + verb** |
| **Starter Ideas** | the dog, my friend, our class, the storm, Maya, the explorers |
| **Examples** | **The dog** chased the ball.<br>**Our class** visited the museum.<br>**Maya** opened the mysterious box.<br>**The storm** shook the windows. |
| **Look For** | The sentence begins with its subject. Use this opener, but do not begin every sentence the same way. |

### 8. Prepositional Opener (P)

| Category | Content |
|---|---|
| **Remember** | Starts with a phrase that tells where, when, or in what direction. |
| **Formula** | **prepositional phrase, + complete thought** |
| **Word Bank** | after, before, beneath, beside, during, inside, near, over, through, under |
| **Examples** | **After lunch**, we played outside.<br>**Beneath the bridge**, the water rushed past.<br>**During the storm**, the lights flickered.<br>**Through the forest**, the hikers followed the trail. |
| **Look For** | An opening phrase beginning with a preposition; usually place a comma after it. |

### 9. -ly Opener (LY)

| Category | Content |
|---|---|
| **Remember** | Starts with an **-ly** adverb that tells how the action happens. |
| **Formula** | **-ly adverb, + complete thought** |
| **Word Bank** | carefully, eagerly, gently, nervously, proudly, quickly, quietly, sadly, slowly, suddenly |
| **Examples** | **Carefully**, Ava carried the glass vase.<br>**Eagerly**, the children opened their gifts.<br>**Quietly**, the fox crept toward the pond.<br>**Suddenly**, thunder shook the house. |
| **Look For** | An **-ly** word at the beginning and a comma after it. |

### 10. -ing Opener (ING)

| Category | Content |
|---|---|
| **Remember** | Starts with an action ending in **-ing**. The person doing that action must come next. |
| **Formula** | **-ing phrase, + doer + verb** |
| **Starter Ideas** | running, smiling, carrying, looking, climbing, hoping, listening, shivering |
| **Examples** | **Running down the path**, Liam chased the bus.<br>**Smiling proudly**, Nora displayed her painting.<br>**Carrying a lantern**, the guide entered the cave.<br>**Shivering in the wind**, the puppy waited by the door. |
| **Look For** | The noun after the comma must be the one doing the **-ing** action. |

### 11. Clausal Opener (CL)

| Category | Content |
|---|---|
| **Remember** | Starts with a dependent clause that tells when, why, where, or under what condition. |
| **Formula** | **dependent clause, + complete thought** |
| **Word Bank** | when, while, where, as, since, if, although, because |
| **Examples** | **Because the road was icy**, school opened late.<br>**If the rain stops**, we will play outside.<br>**While the soup simmered**, Dad baked bread.<br>**Although the puzzle was difficult**, we finished it. |
| **Look For** | A clause that cannot stand alone, followed by a comma and a complete thought. |

### 12. Very Short Sentence (VSS)

| Category | Content |
|---|---|
| **Remember** | A complete sentence of two to five words used for emphasis. |
| **Formula** | **2–5 words + end punctuation** |
| **Starter Ideas** | Everyone froze. It was gone. The door opened. What a surprise! We had won. |
| **Examples** | **Everyone froze.**<br>**The door creaked open.**<br>**It was gone.**<br>**What a surprise!** |
| **Look For** | A complete thought placed where a brief sentence will create strong emphasis. Use sparingly. |

## Quick Writing Checklist

- [ ] I used a quality adjective.
- [ ] I replaced a weak verb with a strong verb.
- [ ] I added an **-ly** adverb where it helps.
- [ ] I explained why with a **because** clause.
- [ ] I added helpful information with a **who/which** clause.
- [ ] I used a WWW.ASIA clause correctly.
- [ ] I varied my sentence openers.
- [ ] I checked that each sentence is complete.
- [ ] I checked capitals, commas, spelling, and end punctuation.

## Common Mistakes

- Use **who** for people and **which** for things or animals.
- Do not add describing words that repeat the same idea.
- In an **-ing** opener, the noun after the comma must perform the opening action.
- A clause beginning with a WWW.ASIA word needs a complete thought beside it.
- Use a VSS for emphasis—not over and over.
- Strong writing is clear; every dress-up should help the reader.
