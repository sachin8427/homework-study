Graphs of Functions

Reading straight-line graphs, matching x and y values, and using a graphed line to solve word problems.

Two Kinds of Lines

Every line in this chapter comes from an equation shaped like y = mx (starts at zero) or y = mx + b (starts somewhere else).

Type 1 — y = mx

Starts at the origin

No number added on. The line always passes through (0, 0) — when x is 0, y is 0 too.

y = 4x

Type 2 — y = mx + b

Starts above or below zero

The "+ b" shifts the whole line up. When x is 0, y is already b — not zero.

y = 3x + 1

Reading the Graph
Pattern A

Given x, find y

Start at your x-value on the bottom axis. Go straight up until you hit the line. Then go straight across to the y-axis and read the number.

Example: on y = 4x, starting at x = 2 and tracing up-then-across lands at y = 8. Check it algebraically too: 4 × 2 = 8. ✓

Pattern B

Given y, find x

Start at your y-value on the side axis. Go straight across until you hit the line. Then go straight down to the x-axis and read the number.

This is the reverse trace of Pattern A — same line, opposite starting point.

Watch out — always double-check with the equation
Watch Out

Graphs can be hard to read exactly

A point that looks like it's at 3.3 might really be at 3 1/3. Whenever a graph reading isn't a clean whole number, plug your x back into the equation to confirm the exact value — don't just eyeball it.

Word Problems from Graphs
Applying the line

The line can represent a real situation

Sometimes x and y stand for real quantities — like the width and length of a rectangle. The equation of the line (e.g. y = 3x + 1) still works exactly the same way; just remember what x and y represent.

Width = x, Length = y = 3x + 1

Find length from width: plug the width in for x, solve for y.
Find width from length: set y equal to the given length, solve the equation for x.
Find perimeter: once you have both x and y, use P = 2(length + width).

Quick Refresher — earlier trouble spots

These patterns showed up on the Chapter 1 & 2 tests and are worth a fast review since they still come up inside word problems here.

Watch Out

Division direction

"How much butter per cake, if 10 kg makes q cakes" is 10 ÷ q, not 10 × q. The total gets split by the number of groups — the variable goes on the bottom.

Watch Out

Two-equal-groups: divide before you subtract

If a total is split evenly between two people and one has a known amount more, subtract that extra first, then divide by 2 — don't subtract straight from the total.
Example: total = n, Sherry made 18 fewer cranes than Ling → Ling's amount is (n + 18) ÷ 2, not n − 18.

Watch Out

Expression vs. equation

"Patrick has y quarters and 8 more dimes than quarters" asks for two separate expressions (quarters = y, dimes = y + 8) — not one equation joining them with an equals sign.

Watch Out

= means equals, not "greater than"

When solving 2a + 3 = 11, the answer is a = 4. Keep the equals sign all the way through — don't switch to a > symbol partway through your work.
