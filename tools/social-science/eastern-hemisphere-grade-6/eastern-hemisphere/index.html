Eastern Hemisphere

Six regions, one big idea: geography shapes how people live, and that shapes what they leave behind. Follow each region from land → civilization → legacy.

Land shapes life, life leaves a legacy

Each region follows the same pattern: geography → the civilization it made possible → what still remains today.

🌿
Africa
Nile Valley & the Sahara
Geography
The Nile River floods on a predictable schedule
Every year the Nile deposits rich black soil on its banks, making farming reliable in the middle of a desert.
which allows ↓
Civilization
Ancient Egypt unites, c. 3100 BCE
Reliable farming supports large populations, cities, and a powerful centralized government under the pharaohs.
leaves behind ↓
Legacy today
Writing, math, and monuments
Hieroglyphics, early geometry for surveying flooded land, and the pyramids are still studied worldwide.
meanwhile, across the desert ↓
Trade connection
Trans-Saharan caravan routes
Camel caravans crossed the Sahara carrying West African gold north and salt south, building wealthy kingdoms like Mali — whose ruler Mansa Musa made Timbuktu a center of learning.
🏭
Middle East
The Fertile Crescent
Geography
Two rivers, unpredictable floods
The Tigris and Euphrates make farming possible in dry Mesopotamia, but their floods are far less predictable than the Nile's.
which requires ↓
Civilization
Sumer builds the first cities, c. 3500 BCE
Managing irrigation and unpredictable flooding requires organization — leading to some of the world's first cities and governments.
leaves behind ↓
Legacy today
Writing and written law
Cuneiform is one of the first writing systems, and Hammurabi's Code (c. 1750 BCE) is one of the first written sets of laws — the ancestor of legal systems today.
and religiously ↓
Cultural legacy
Birthplace of three major world religions
Judaism, Christianity, and Islam all trace their origins to this region, still shaping cultures across the world today.
🏠
South Asia
Indus & Ganges river valleys
Geography
Monsoon rains and two great rivers
The Indus and Ganges rivers, fed by seasonal monsoon rains, support farming for enormous populations — but too little or too much rain can mean famine or flood.
which supports ↓
Civilization
The Indus Valley Civilization, c. 2500 BCE
Cities like Mohenjo-daro had advanced city planning and drainage systems centuries ahead of their time. Their script still hasn't been fully deciphered.
leaves behind ↓
Legacy today
Religion and mathematics
Hinduism and Buddhism both originate here and spread across Asia. The concept of zero as a number was developed in India — a foundation for all modern math.
🏜
East Asia
Yellow & Yangtze river valleys
Geography
Mountains and deserts isolate the region
Natural barriers on nearly every side let China develop a distinct culture with less outside interference than other early civilizations.
while inside, ↓
Civilization
Early dynasties rise along the Yellow River, c. 2000 BCE
Frequent flooding of the Yellow River required massive coordinated labor for water control — favoring strong, centralized rule.
leaves behind ↓
Legacy today
Confucian values and world-changing inventions
Confucianism's emphasis on respect for elders and education still shapes East Asian culture. Paper, printing, the compass, and gunpowder all originated here and later spread west along trade routes.
🏠
Southeast Asia
Peninsulas & islands between two oceans
Geography
A crossroads between India and China
Monsoon winds reverse direction with the seasons, letting sailors ride them one way to India and the other way back — making this region a natural trading crossroads.
which brings in ↓
Cultural exchange
Hindu and Buddhist influence arrives by trade, not conquest
Traders and travelers, not armies, carried religion and art styles from India — blending with local traditions rather than replacing them.
leaves behind ↓
Legacy today
Angkor Wat
The Khmer Empire (c. 800–1400 CE) built Angkor Wat, one of the largest religious monuments in the world, blending Hindu and later Buddhist styles.
🏞
Pacific Islands
The widest ocean on Earth
Geography
Thousands of islands, vast distances
Some islands are thousands of miles from the nearest land — there was no coastline to follow, only open ocean.
which demands ↓
Civilization
Polynesian voyaging
Navigators crossed thousands of miles of open ocean reading stars, wave patterns, and bird flight — with no compass — deliberately settling islands like Hawaii, New Zealand, and Easter Island.
leaves behind ↓
Legacy today
Oral tradition and island self-sufficiency
Navigation knowledge passed down through spoken tradition, not writing. Each island group adapted independently to its own limited land and resources.
Memory tricks

Real memory devices for the parts of this unit that are hardest to keep straight.

Order of the great river civilizations
"My Elephant Is Crying"
Oldest to youngest: Mesopotamia (c. 3500 BCE) → Egypt (c. 3100 BCE) → Indus Valley (c. 2500 BCE) → China (c. 2000 BCE). All four grew up around rivers, in almost that exact order.
The six regions, in study order
"Ancient Maps Show Every Sea Path"
Africa · Middle East · South Asia · East Asia · Southeast Asia · Pacific Islands. Say the phrase, and you've got the region order.
Three types of economy
Tribe. Crown. Money.
Traditional economy → decisions made by Tribe/custom (how your family has always farmed). Command economy → decisions made by the Crown or government (a ruler decides what's grown and traded). Market economy → decisions made by Money — buyers and sellers, supply and demand.
Zero's journey around the world
"India births it, Arabia carries it, Europe names it"
The concept of zero was developed in India, adopted and spread by scholars in the Arab world, and reached Europe so thoroughly through Arab traders that we still call our numbers "Arabic numerals" today, even though the idea started in India.
